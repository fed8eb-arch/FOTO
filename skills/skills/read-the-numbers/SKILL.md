# Read the numbers

The owner does not need another chart. He needs to know what moved, what it means, and what to do once.

Build or open the source first:

- Sales: follow `sales-reporting-dashboard`.
- The whole business: follow `kpi-dashboard`.
- Stock and the product list: follow `inventory-manager`.
- The books: follow `bookkeeping-categorization`.
- Profit, the balance, and cash as three statements: follow `data-financial-analysis`.

Then read. Do not add a second dashboard.

## What a number is allowed to be

A figure comes from a file, a page, or a ledger the project already has. If the input is missing, the cell stays blank and the blank is named. A forecast needs history. A stock figure needs a count. A price needs a decision, not a guess copied from a sample.

## The three lines

For the page in front of him, write only these three lines. One each.

1. What moved. Name the number, the period, and the direction. "Orders fell from 40 to 28 in the last 30 days" is a reading. "Sales are soft" is not.
2. What it means. Translate it into customers or money. Profit on the income line and cash in the bank are different facts. A full warehouse with no orders is cash locked in boxes. A rising refund rate eats the revenue the sales chart just celebrated.
3. The one decision. One action, this week, that he or the product can take. If the number does not justify a change, the decision is to leave it and name the date to look again.

If two numbers fight, say which one he would feel in the bank account. That is the one that leads.

## The few comparisons that change a mind

Use only the pairs that are both present.

- Revenue against cash. A profitable month can still empty the account.
- New customers against the cost to get one, and against what one is worth. If the cost or the worth is blank, say the pair cannot be judged yet.
- Orders against stock. Selling what is not there, or storing what does not sell.
- Price against refunds. A higher price that comes back is not a raise.
- This period against the previous one of the same length. A single day is not a trend.

## What not to hand him

A wall of ratios. A benchmark from another company. A red-yellow-green painting with no sentence under it. A recommendation to buy a tool. The reading fits on one screen.
