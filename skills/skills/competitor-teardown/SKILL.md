# Competitor teardown

Open the live pages. Record only what is on them, with the URL and the date. Use web search and the browser already in Cursor. Do not install another research CLI. Stop at 5 direct competitors. A missing number stays missing.

Write the result into `docs/market.md`. Lead with the one gap Peter can own.

## Seven layers

| Layer | Look at | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Product | Features, UX, quality | The product and screenshots |
| Pricing | Plans, model, hidden costs | The pricing page |
| Positioning | Message, tagline, who it is for | Homepage and ads |
| Traction | Users, revenue, growth | Press and filings, only if stated |
| Reviews | What users praise and hate | G2, Capterra, app stores, Reddit |
| Content | Blog, social, search topics | Their site and profiles |
| Team | Size, notable hires | About page, LinkedIn |

## Feature matrix

| Feature | Ours | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name | yes, paid only, or no | | | |

Mark full, partial, or absent. Note the condition: paid only, enterprise, beta. Lead with features where this product wins. State competitor strengths plainly.

## SWOT and map

One SWOT per competitor: strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats. Then a 2x2 on two axes that change a buying decision, such as simple against complex, or self-serve against sales-led. Do not use good against bad, or cheap against expensive.

## Reviews

From G2, Capterra, the app stores, Product Hunt, or Reddit, extract only repeated points: most praised, most complained, why people switch, requested features, and comparisons they name.

## Close

One page: the category, the 3 to 5 names, where this product sits, and one sentence on the opportunity. Then the comparison table. End each section with what it means for this product. Date the file.
