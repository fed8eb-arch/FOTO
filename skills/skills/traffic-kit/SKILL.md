# Traffic kit

One product, one asset, then the cuts. A factory that invents a new article, video, and reel every day is the wrong machine. Traffic comes from a few pieces people can find, cite, and share.

## Before writing

Read `docs/brief.md`, `docs/market.md`, and `.agents/product-marketing.md` when it exists. If the brief or the market file is missing, stop and fill it. The visitor, the offer, and the proof have to be real. If the positioning document does not exist, follow `product-marketing` before writing.

Follow `content-strategy` only far enough to name at most three pieces. Recommend one. Show Peter the slate: title, the query it should rank or be cited for, and why it can be shared. Wait for a yes. Do not write the set in the same turn as the slate.

## After a yes

For the approved piece, in order:

1. `seo-content-brief` for that one page.
2. `copywriting` from that brief. No invented numbers, quotes, or customers.
3. `schema` if the page is an article, product, or offer.
4. `ai-seo` only as a pass on the finished draft: a direct answer up top, a dated source, an author. Do not rewrite it into a second "AI version."
5. `social` cuts the same piece into posts and a reel script. `contagious` scores whether there is a reason to share it. If the score is weak, say so and change the hook. Do not film a different idea.

Save the page in the project and the slate plus cuts in `docs/traffic.md`.

## Video

A reel script and a shot list are the default. When the piece needs the video file, remind Peter once that he or the product can generate it, then follow `video` only after he says to. A flagship film for the site still goes through `10k-websites`. Do not spend on a video for every post.

## Pages at scale

Use `programmatic-seo` only when the product has a real table of distinct facts, such as cities or integrations. A table of reworded articles is not that.
